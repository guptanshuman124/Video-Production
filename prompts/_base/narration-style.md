# NARRATION STYLE

- Sound like a friendly, clear teacher talking to one student — not a textbook read aloud. Plain words, contractions ("let's", "it's", "here's"), sentences of about 12–20 words.
- One new idea, term or number per sentence. State it, let it land, then explain it in the next sentence.
- After a key fact or result, pause with a short standalone sentence or a trailing "—" before explaining.
- Make it interactive: address the student ("notice that…", "think about what happens if…") and ask one or two genuine questions per slide.
- The slide is a summary; the narration teaches. Never read the slide text out word for word — explain it, give the reason, add an NCERT example, connect it to what came before.
- Reach the word range through real depth: a second NCERT example, the reason a structure or step exists, a likely confusion cleared up, how the board exam asks about it. Never pad by repeating yourself.
- On question slides, dismantle the tempting wrong option briefly, then explain the right one. Do not walk through every option.

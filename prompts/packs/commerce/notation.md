# COMMERCE NOTATION

- **Money:** always rupees, written with ₹ and Indian grouping in slide text ("₹1,25,000", "₹12,000"). Never the dollar sign — `$…$` is maths on these slides.
- **Amount fields** (journal lines, ledger rows, trial balance, balance sheet, final accounts) are plain numbers: `25000`, not "25,000" or "₹25,000". The slide formats them and adds every total itself — never write a Total line.
- **Balance every account:** journal debits = credits per entry; ledger Dr side = Cr side (close with "Balance c/d", open the next period with "Balance b/d"); trial balance and balance sheet totals agree; Trading / P&L close with "Gross Profit c/d", "Gross Profit b/d", "Net Profit". The slide refuses anything that does not balance.
- **Account names** as NCERT writes them, ending in "A/c" in the Journal ("Furniture A/c", "Ramesh's A/c", "Rent A/c"). Do not add "Dr.", "To" or "By" — the slide adds them in the right place.
- **Dates** as in the question ("01-04-2024" or "2024 April 1"); the accounting year is "for the year ended 31st March 2025", the Balance Sheet "as at 31st March 2025".
- **Formulas and ratios** inside `$…$` with words as `\text{…}`: $\text{Current Ratio} = \dfrac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$.
- **Narration** says amounts in words in the Indian system ("pachchees hazaar rupaye", "ek lakh pachchees hazaar"), dates naturally ("first April"), and reads "A/c" as "account", "Dr." as "debit", "c/d" as "carried down", "b/d" as "brought down".

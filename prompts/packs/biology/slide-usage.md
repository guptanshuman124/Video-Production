# WHICH SLIDE TYPE FOR WHICH BIOLOGY CONTENT

- **chapter_index** — lecture 1 only, first slide: the chapter name and its main topics (3–10).
- **definition** — a named concept, process, principle or structure: osmosis, homeostasis, double fertilisation, transpiration, ecological succession, a reflex arc. Add a small table only for the concept's own types.
- **characteristics** — 4–6 genuine properties of one thing: features of Chordata, a xerophyte, enzymes, C4 plants. Fewer than 4 real points → fold them into a definition instead.
- **comparison** — two entities across 3–6 bases: mitosis vs meiosis, xylem vs phloem, monocot vs dicot, DNA vs RNA, innate vs acquired immunity. Three entities only when NCERT itself tabulates three.
- **labeled_diagram** — the workhorse: an organ or organelle structure, a life cycle, stages of division, a pathway, a classification chart, a pedigree, a food web, a technique flowchart — whenever a catalog figure shows it.
- **image_points** — a figure or graph with 2–5 observations: a growth curve, enzyme activity vs temperature, a labelled structure with the functions of its parts.
- **mechanism** — how something works in 2–3 stages beside a figure: how the nephron forms urine, how a nerve impulse travels.
- **solved_example** — genetics or ecology numericals only; sparingly.
- **descriptive_answer** — a board-style short/long-answer question whose answer is a chain of 2–5 points: "Why is meiosis important for variation? (3 marks)".
- **mcq** — recall, structure-based (with the figure), identify the part, predict the cross, odd one out, arrange the stages.
- **assertion_reason** — only a genuine claim-and-cause pair from this content.
- **misconception** — only real, frequent confusions from this content ("all bacteria are harmful", "genes and chromosomes are the same thing").
- **quick_revision** — recap of what this lecture already taught: key points plus glossary. Never introduces anything new.

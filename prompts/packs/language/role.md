# SUBJECT — {{subject}} (LANGUAGE)

- This is a **language lesson**, not a lesson about facts. Students learn {{subject}} by reading it, hearing it explained, using its words and grammar, and writing it. Teach the way a good {{subject}} teacher teaches the class: read the text aloud, explain it line by line, open up the difficult words, show what makes the writing beautiful, and bring out its message.
- **The text is the lesson.** Quote the textbook's lines exactly — every word, every spelling, every punctuation mark. Never paraphrase a line and call it a quote, never translate a quoted line, never invent a line the text does not have.
- **Teach in the language of the subject.** A Hindi lesson is taught in Hindi; an English lesson in English (see LANGUAGE). Slide text is in the same language.
- Keep NCERT's order: the lesson unfolds as the text does. A lecture covers its part of the text closely rather than skimming the whole chapter.
- CBSE answer-writing matters: line explanation with context (सप्रसंग व्याख्या / reference to context), character sketches proved from the text, the central idea, extract-based questions, and grammar in the board's question forms.

What a language lesson is made of:
- **Literature (prose, poetry, drama):** what the text is about → the author or poet → reading the text closely, part by part (`passage`) with its difficult words (`word_meanings`) → characters (`character`) → devices and beauty (`literary_device`) → the message (`central_idea`) → questions.
- **Grammar (व्याकरण):** the idea in everyday use → the rule with its pattern (`grammar_rule`) → examples → practice with answers (`grammar_practice`) → common mistakes (`misconception`).
- **Writing skills (रचना):** the purpose → the format (`writing_format`) → a model → practice.

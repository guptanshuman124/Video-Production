# SUBJECT — MATHEMATICS

- Mathematics is learnt by seeing why and then doing. Every new idea gets a plain-language lead-in (a situation, a pattern, a question), then its exact NCERT definition or statement, then worked examples, then practice.
- Accuracy is non-negotiable: every number, sign, step and answer must be right and must follow from the previous step. Use NCERT's notation, terms and methods, not alternative ones.
- Show the working a student writes in the exam: the formula first, then substitution, then simplification, then the answer (with its unit when there is one). Never skip the step an examiner looks for.
- Geometry lives in figures: a theorem, construction or problem about a figure is shown with its NCERT figure when the catalog has one.
- Classes 6–8: activity first — let the student try, notice or measure before a term is named. Classes 9–12: state precisely, prove what NCERT proves, and practise the CBSE question patterns.

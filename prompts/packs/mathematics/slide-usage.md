# WHICH SLIDE TYPE FOR WHICH MATHS CONTENT

- **intro** — every lecture, first slide only: a title card (chapter name, "Lecture N: title") filled in by code. Nothing to write; the narration is a ~10-second welcome.
- **hook** — lecture 1 only, second slide: a real-world scenario or puzzle that shows why the chapter matters (a savings plan for APs, a ladder against a wall for trigonometry) — no formal maths yet.
- **concept_intro** — right before a new term, formula or theorem: the plain situation or pattern that makes it needed. Usually no image.
- **definition** — a new term: formal definition, 1–3 properties or types, optional small table, and its formula / notation with a symbol key. An image only for geometric terms (chord, tangent, radian, secant).
- **theorem** — a result stated formally: the title names it; statement (+ formula for algebraic results); Given / To prove for geometric theorems. Always followed by its `proof` when NCERT proves it.
- **proof** — the step-by-step proof of the theorem on the previous slide, 2–6 steps with reasons; the figure when there is one. Never standalone.
- **solved_example** — a worked problem: the problem, 2–6 solution steps, the answer, optionally the common mistake. The most frequent slide in a maths lecture; use NCERT examples or ones of the same pattern. With the figure for geometry / graph problems.
- **labeled_diagram** — a construction, graph or figure that carries the explanation on its own, read part by part.
- **comparison** — genuinely tabular content: a table of values (trigonometric ratios, a frequency table) or 2–3 things compared across bases (rational vs irrational numbers).
- **try_this** — an activity before a concept is formalised (fold, measure, draw, spot the pattern) — no answer shown. Especially Classes 6–8.
- **practice_problem** — 1–3 exam-style problems for the student to solve, answers withheld; a checkpoint after one or two worked examples.
- **misconception** — real, frequent errors: `$(a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$`, adding denominators, dividing by a variable that can be zero.
- **historical_note** — optional, at most once in a chapter: a mathematician or origin story that NCERT mentions (Aryabhata and zero, Euclid, Ramanujan).
- **mcq** — conceptual or one-step numerical, with one trap option (the classic slip: sign, formula, unit).
- **assertion_reason** — a genuine claim-and-reason pair from this content (Classes 10–12 especially).
- **quick_revision** — end of the lecture: key points (formulas as `$…$`) + glossary.

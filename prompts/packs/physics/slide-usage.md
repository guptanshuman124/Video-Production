# WHICH SLIDE TYPE FOR WHICH PHYSICS CONTENT

- **intro** — every lecture, first slide only: a title card (chapter name, "Lecture N: title") filled in by code. Nothing to write; the narration is a ~10-second welcome.
- **definition** — a quantity, law or concept (velocity, Ohm's law, electric flux): one-line definition, 1–3 properties (scalar/vector, unit, dimension), optional small table, and **its formula with a symbol key** when one exists.
- **characteristics** — 4–6 genuine properties of one thing: properties of electric charge, of a real image, of electromagnetic waves.
- **comparison** — two (at most three) things across 3–6 bases: distance vs displacement, series vs parallel, real vs virtual image, AC vs DC.
- **labeled_diagram** — one large figure read part by part: ray diagram, circuit, apparatus, field-line pattern, graph.
- **image_points** — a graph or figure with 2–5 observations, each with its formula if any (v–t graph: slope = acceleration, area = displacement).
- **mechanism** — how a device or phenomenon works in 2–3 stages beside a figure: galvanometer, transformer, AC generator, total internal reflection.
- **derivation** — an NCERT derivation in 2–4 steps building to a stated equation: equations of motion, lens formula, expression for capacitance.
- **solved_example** — a numerical: given values, formula → substitution → answer with unit. Use NCERT examples or ones of the same pattern.
- **descriptive_answer** — a board short/long-answer question answered as 2–5 reasoning lines + takeaway ("Why does a charged comb attract paper? (2 marks)").
- **mcq** — conceptual, formula-based or one-step numerical, with one trap option (the classic mistake: wrong sign, wrong unit, confusing mass and weight).
- **assertion_reason** — a genuine claim-and-cause pair from this content.
- **misconception** — real, frequent confusions ("heavier bodies fall faster", "current is used up in a bulb").
- **formula_sheet** — end of a formula-heavy lecture: the formulas taught, each labelled, plus a symbol key.
- **quick_revision** — end of a concept-heavy lecture: key points + glossary of what was taught.

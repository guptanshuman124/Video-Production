# PHYSICS NOTATION

**Formulas are LaTeX.** In slide text write inline maths as `$…$` (e.g. `$F = ma$`); in a `formula`, `goal` or formula-sheet field write bare LaTeX without `$`. Use NCERT symbols: `v`, `u`, `a`, `s`, `t`, `F`, `m`, `W`, `P`, `E`, `\vec{F}` for vectors, `\Delta` for change, `\propto` for proportional.

**Units** in SI, written as NCERT does: `m s^{-1}`, `N`, `J`, `W`, `\Omega`, `C`, `V`, `A`, `T`. Powers of ten as `\times 10^{-11}`. Every numerical answer carries a unit.

**Derivations** (derivation slide): 2–4 steps, one equation per step, each step a real algebraic move stated in words ("substitute", "integrate", "rearrange"). End exactly on the NCERT result given as the goal. Never skip the step a board examiner looks for.

**Numericals** (solved_example slide): the problem restates all given values with units; steps go formula → substitution with numbers → result; the final answer is one value with its unit, rounded as NCERT does.

**Say it, don't show LaTeX in narration.** The voice-over reads formulas in words ("F equals m a", "v square equals u square plus two a s", "ten to the power minus eleven"). No `$`, backslashes or symbols in narration.

**Diagrams** (ray diagrams, circuits, field lines, graphs, apparatus) are images — never describe them as a run of text lines; use a figure slide when a catalog image shows them.
